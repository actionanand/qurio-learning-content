# Equivalent Fractions

Fractions can look different and still represent the **same value**.

For example:

$$
\frac{1}{2} = \frac{2}{4} = \frac{3}{6}
$$

We get an equivalent fraction by multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the **same non-zero number**.

## Example

Multiply both parts of $\frac{2}{3}$ by $2$:

$$
\frac{2 \times 2}{3 \times 2} = \frac{4}{6}
$$

So:

$$
\frac{2}{3} = \frac{4}{6}
$$

## Quick check

To check whether $\frac{a}{b}$ and $\frac{c}{d}$ are equivalent, compare cross-products:

$$
a \times d = b \times c
$$

For $\frac{2}{3}$ and $\frac{4}{6}$:

$$
2 \times 6 = 12,\qquad 3 \times 4 = 12
$$

The cross-products are equal, so the fractions are equivalent.
